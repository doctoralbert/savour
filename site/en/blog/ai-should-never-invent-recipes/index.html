Why AI should never invent your recipes

AI is good at filling in what is probably there. In a kitchen, “probably” is not good enough.

23 October 2026 · 2 min read

In short

  • A clever guess looks just as convincing as the right answer.
  • Savour never invents quantities, ingredients or steps: what is not there stays empty.
  • Doubt is marked, and the chef decides.

The problem with guessing

Read a blurry photo of a note and you might guess it says 150 grams of sugar. But if it was 150 grams of glucose, your marshmallow is no longer right. A smart guess looks just as convincing as the right answer.

What Savour does differently

Savour never invents quantities, ingredients or steps. If the method is not on the note, it is not in the recipe either. You add it yourself.

What a guess does
  • fills an unreadable number with what is usual
  • writes a method when there is none
  • looks convincing, even when it is wrong
What Savour does
  • takes over what is there
  • leaves empty what is not there
  • marks what is uncertain, so you can check

Making doubt visible

When the reader or the AI is not sure, you see it. The recipe is flagged and you check it before it is saved. Better a question to you than a mistake on the plate.

Better a question to you than a mistake on the plate.

The chef decides

AI is a tool to work faster, not a replacement for craft. The last word on a recipe always belongs to the cook who made it.

What AI is good at

AI is strong at reading: messy handwriting, crooked photos and documents without a clear structure. It saves you retyping. But reading is different from filling in. A good process keeps the two strictly apart: what is written is copied, what is not written stays empty.

How to check an imported recipe

Look at the numbers first, then the units, then the yield. Read the method through once. If something seems wrong, compare it with the original; for a handwritten note the photo is kept. Only then make a recipe available to your team.

This is one of Savour’s core values: a recipe has to be right.
