Allergens in Savour: how the new feature will work

A guest asks at the table whether there are nuts in the sauce. With the new allergen feature, the answer is in the recipe, even when the nuts are in a base recipe. This is how it works, and how it deliberately does not.

6 November 2026 · 5 min read

In short

  • Savour adds up the allergens of every recipe, including those from your base recipes.
  • You link hundreds of ingredient names in one go, and you always see first what Savour will do.
  • As long as something is unchecked, Savour says so. Never "allergen-free" by guesswork.

From ingredient to product

It starts with a list of products: ingredients as your kitchen buys them, such as butter, flour or soy sauce. Each product has its allergens. Savour links recipe lines to a product by name: in "250 g cold butter, diced" Savour recognises the product Butter. That way Savour adds up the allergens of each recipe.

If you use a base recipe, such as a stock, a dough or a sauce, the allergens of that base recipe count too. If the mustard is in your vinaigrette, you also see mustard on the salad that uses it.

The 14 allergens, and what guests ask about

Savour works with the 14 allergens from the European rules: gluten, crustaceans, eggs, fish, peanuts, soy, milk, nuts, celery, mustard, sesame, sulphites, lupin and molluscs. You can also track extra characteristics guests ask about, for example lactose, pork, alcohol, the onion family, garlic, mushrooms or FODMAP. You choose whether you want only the 14 legal allergens or the extras too.

Lactose is deliberately separate from milk: lactose-free cream still contains milk protein.

No linking hundreds of names by hand

A recipe book with hundreds of recipes quickly has a thousand different ingredient names. Nobody wants to link them one by one. With Link everything automatically, Savour handles them in one go:

  • a base recipe with the same name ("veg stock" becomes your recipe Vegetable stock);
  • a product you already have, also with a different spelling or in the plural;
  • the main product: "kale without stalk" is Kale, "skinless cod fillet" is Cod;
  • a new product from Savour’s ingredient list, with the allergens as a suggestion;
  • if there is doubt, as with "juice" or "coulis", Savour leaves the name as it is. Which juice? Only you know.

You always see first what Savour will do

Before anything changes, you see the whole list: which name goes to which product and which allergens that product gets. Anything you untick, Savour leaves alone. Afterwards you can still adjust every product. Savour never quietly changes your recipes: the recipe lines themselves stay exactly as you wrote them.

If you do not want to start from zero, take over the starter list: over 200 products that appear most often in professional recipes. For stock, gelatine and chocolate Savour asks a question first, because it depends on what your kitchen uses.

Start with your base recipes

Once the allergens of your stocks, doughs and sauces are right, they are right in every dish that uses them. That is where you save the most time.

The ingredient list Savour reads

The suggestions come from one list of over 3,700 ingredients, built from four sources: the ingredient list of Open Food Facts, the Dutch Wikipedia lists of vegetables, herbs, mushrooms, sauces and cheeses, the Dutch product names from NEVO (the Dutch food composition database of RIVM) and Savour’s own list of kitchen terms. NEVO itself contains no allergens; those are added by Savour. The names are in Dutch, with English names where the source has them.

The whole list is public, with the allergens Savour suggests: see Savour’s ingredient list. That way you can check where a suggestion comes from. Spot a mistake? Let us know.

Why Savour never says something is allergen-free

One allergen too many is annoying; one too few is dangerous. That is why Savour is strict. As long as a recipe has lines without a checked product, it says "Not complete yet", with the number of ingredients still open. Only when every line is checked and none of the 14 allergens is in it does Savour say the recipe contains none of the 14 allergens. For the extra characteristics Savour only reports what is in it, never what is not.

A suggestion is only a suggestion. Your supplier’s label always comes first: another brand of stock can contain something slightly different.

One allergen too many is annoying; one too few is dangerous.

New brand, check again

When you switch supplier or brand, check the product in Savour again. A different stock cube can contain something slightly different.

In the kitchen team and on paper

A kitchen team has one product list for the whole team. The owner and the sous-chefs manage that list; a cook sees the allergens on every recipe and can see per line where they come from. When you print a recipe, the allergens are on the print, and if the recipe is not fully checked yet, that is on it too.

When is it coming?

The allergen feature now runs in our test version, with real recipe books from the kitchen. We will put it live once the linking is good enough for the rush of a service. Tracking allergens will be part of Savour Pro, as well as in a kitchen team whose owner has Pro.

This explains the feature as it works in the test version today. If anything changes at launch, we will update it here.
