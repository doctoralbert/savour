Recipe card template: what a standard recipe sheet needs

A recipe card is the recipe as your kitchen makes it every day: fixed quantities, a fixed yield and the points where it can go wrong. This is what belongs on it, with a blank template to copy.

SAVOUR

23 November 2026 · by Jop Schreurs · 5 min read

In short

  • A recipe card always has a name, a yield, ingredients with quantity and unit, and the method in steps.
  • Add the critical points (temperature, cooking) and the allergens, and a date, so you can see which version is on the wall.
  • Large, calm and in the same layout for every recipe: then everyone reads it at a glance.

What a recipe card is

A recipe tells you how to make something. A recipe card records how your kitchen makes it: the same every time, by everyone. It is the fixed version the whole brigade works from, and the basis for scaling, allergens and later the cost price. Why that matters so much: Standardised recipes.

What belongs on it

These are the fields of a good recipe card, and why they matter.

FieldWhy
NameOne fixed name, so everyone means the same recipe.
YieldFor example 2 litres or 40 portions. Without a yield you cannot scale reliably.
Category or sectionWhere the recipe belongs: sauce, garde manger, pastry.
IngredientsEach with quantity and unit, in grams or millilitres where you can.
MethodIn numbered steps, in the order you work.
Critical pointsCore temperature, cooking, chilling, shelf life.
AllergensWhich of the 14 it contains, including through base recipes.
PhotoWhat it should look like when it is done.
Date or versionSo you can see whether the print on the wall is still right.

A blank template

Copy this layout into your own template. Keep the order the same for every recipe: then nobody has to search.

Recipe card

Name: …

Yield: … portions / … kg / … litres

Section or category: …

Ingredients: quantity · unit · ingredient

Method: 1. … 2. … 3. …

Critical points: core temperature …, cooking …, chilling …

Allergens: …

Date or version: …

Filled in: Beurre blanc from the Savour demo

Name: Beurre blanc

Yield: 4 portions

Section or category: Sauces

Ingredients: 2 shallots, finely chopped · 1 decilitre dry white wine · 5 tablespoons white wine vinegar · 250 grams cold butter, diced · salt to taste · white pepper to taste

Method: 1. Bring the shallot, wine and vinegar to the boil and reduce until about 2 tablespoons of liquid remain. 2. Turn the heat down low and whisk in the butter cube by cube until the sauce is bound and glossy. 3. Do not let it boil again; pass through a fine sieve and season with salt and pepper. 4. Serve straight away or keep warm in a bain-marie for up to an hour.

Critical points: core temperature not applicable · do not let it boil once the butter is in, or it will split · up to an hour in a bain-marie (in Savour as a note on the recipe)

Allergens: milk, sulphites

Date or version: the print date at the bottom of the sheet

Quantities everyone reads the same way

Write quantities in grams and millilitres, not “a splash” or “to taste”, except where it is really about tasting. If you use a base recipe such as a stock or a dough, refer to it instead of writing it out again. If a recipe has parts, such as dough and filling, give each part its own ingredients and method under its own heading.

Ingredients next to the method

Put the ingredients on the left and the method on the right. Then you see at a glance what you need for each step while you work.

Readable in the kitchen

A recipe card hangs on the wall or sits in a folder at the pass. Use large type and plenty of white space, so you can read it from a metre away. Print on A4 for the folder or A5 for a small binder, and put it in a sleeve against stains.

Keeping it up to date

The most dangerous recipe card is an old one. Put a date or version on it, agree when the folders are renewed, for example at every menu change, and take the old print down as soon as the new one is up. Two versions side by side lead to mistakes.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers to what chefs ask most.

Is a recipe card the same as a spec sheet?

Almost. A spec sheet often also has the cost per portion. The recipe card above is the basis; you add the cost once the quantities are right.

Should the allergens be on the recipe card?

It is wise. The allergens are only right if the recipe is right, so they belong with the recipe. Read also Allergen information in restaurants: the rules.

How often should I renew recipe cards?

Every time the recipe changes, and agree a fixed moment to go through everything, for example at every menu change.
