Free food cost calculator: plate cost, food cost and menu price

Enter your ingredients and see right away what a portion costs, which price fits your target and what you keep per plate. Free, no account. Below you read how to use the result.

SAVOUR

24 November 2026 · by Jop Schreurs · 4 min read

In short

  • The calculator works with yield: what you trim, you pay for too.
  • Choose your target food cost and see the suggested price before and with tax.
  • Enter your own menu price and see your food cost and the margin per plate in money.

The calculator

Start with a dish and the number of portions. Not sure where to start? Choose Load example.

How to use it

From purchase price to menu price in four steps.

  1. IngredientsPer ingredient the quantity in the recipe, the purchase price and what that price is for (per kilo, litre or piece).
  2. YieldThe share left after trimming or boning. If you get 850 grams from a kilo, enter 85.
  3. PortionsHow many portions the recipe makes. The calculator divides the cost by it.
  4. Target and priceSlide your target food cost and read the suggested price, or enter your own menu price for your food cost and margin.

Start with the plate cost

It all starts with what one portion costs: every ingredient with its quantity, the purchase price and the yield after trimming, added up. How to work that out, with a full example, is in How to calculate food cost. Here we carry on where that article stops: what do you charge for that plate?

From plate cost to selling price

First choose how much of the price may go to ingredients: your target food cost. Divide the plate cost by it and you have the selling price before tax.

Worked example

Plate cost steak and fries: €6.40

Target food cost: 30%

6.40 ÷ 0.30 = €21.33 before tax

Chosen price before tax: €21.50 → 6.40 ÷ 21.50 = 29.8% food cost

An example, not a norm

The amounts and the 30% are a worked example. Which percentage suits your place depends on your concept, your menu and your other costs.

Tax and rounding

Always calculate before tax: it is not yours. Then add the sales tax that applies in your country. Round to a price that fits your menu and check the food cost percentage once more, because rounding changes it slightly.

Look at money, not only at percentages

A low food cost percentage feels good, but you pay the rent in money. Compare two dishes from the same example:

DishPlate costPrice before taxFood costMargin per plate
Steak and fries€6.40€21.5029.8%€15.10
Tomato pasta€2.10€14.5014.5%€12.40

Worked example. The pasta has the lowest percentage, but the steak leaves more per plate.

Margin per plate

Margin per plate = selling price before tax − plate cost. As a percentage: margin ÷ selling price × 100, which is 100% minus your food cost. You only see which dishes carry your menu when you put both side by side: how much a plate earns and how often it sells.

When to recalculate

A price that was right last year may not be right now. Recalculate a dish when:

  • your supplier changes the price of an important ingredient;
  • the recipe changes, even if it is only a different garnish;
  • the portion gets bigger or smaller;
  • at an agreed moment, for example at every menu change.

Delivery and takeaway

If you also sell through a delivery platform, add the commission and the packaging to your plate cost for that channel. The same dish can have a good margin at the table and earn almost nothing through delivery.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers to what chefs ask most.

How do I calculate the selling price of a dish?

Divide the plate cost by your target food cost (as a number, so 30% = 0.30). That is the price before tax. Add the tax and round.

What is the difference between food cost and margin?

Food cost is the share of the price that goes to ingredients; the margin is what is left per plate. Together they are 100% of the price before tax.

Do I calculate with or without tax?

Without tax, on both sides: purchase prices without tax and the selling price without tax.

How often should I recalculate my prices?

With every price change of an important ingredient, every recipe change and at a fixed moment, such as a new menu.
