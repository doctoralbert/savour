From Word document to recipe book in a few minutes

Many chefs already have it: one big Word file with years of recipes. Retyping takes weeks. It does not have to.

23 October 2026 · 2 min read

In short

  • Choose your Word file or PDF: the Savour reader splits it into separate recipes itself.
  • Titles, quantities and steps are recognised; doubt is marked.
  • Free up to 100 recipes; with Pro unlimited, and the AI also reads photos.

Drop the file in

Choose your Word file or PDF in Savour. The Savour reader goes through the whole document and splits it into separate recipes itself, even if there are hundreds.

  1. Choose a fileUnder “Add recipe” you choose the Word file, PDF or text.
  2. The reader splitsSavour goes through the whole document and turns it into separate recipes.
  3. CheckYou see all recipes in one overview; you check what is marked.
  4. SaveSave everything at once, then put it in folders and share.

What the reader recognises

Titles, also when bold or numbered, ingredients with quantity and unit, and the method in steps. Numbers before titles and small typos, such as 18oc instead of 180°C, are cleaned up.

What the reader tidies up

“12. Veal jus” becomes the title Veal jus

“oven at 18oc” becomes 180°C

a recipe that runs on halfway down the page stays one recipe

When the reader is unsure

Not every document is neatly formatted. If the reader is unsure about a recipe, it is flagged and you check it yourself before it is saved. With Savour Pro the AI reads those doubtful cases automatically.

How fast it is

One chef imported 1,400 recipes from Word this way in about two minutes. Then you can put them in folders, complete them and share them straight away.

1,400recipes from Word, read in in about two minutes.

Free or Pro

You import Word, PDF and text for free with the Savour reader, up to 100 recipes. With Savour Pro you store unlimited recipes and the AI also reads photos and handwritten notes.

Tidy your file a little first

You do not need to retype anything, but a few small steps help. Put each recipe name on its own line, use headings such as "Ingredients" and "Method" if you already have them, and remove blank pages. The clearer the structure, the less you check afterwards.

After importing

First go through the recipes Savour marked. Then put the most used recipes into folders and carry on from there. You do not have to organise everything in one day; recipes not yet sorted stay findable through search and A to Z.

You find importing in Savour under “Add recipe”.
